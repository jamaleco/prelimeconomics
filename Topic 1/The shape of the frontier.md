While many previous examples ([[New resources and the frontier]]) are straight lines, many real world frontiers are not. A straight line frontier means that the [[production]] of goods can be exchanged at a constant rate. This is unrealistic because leather and oil are typically made by different resources and thus switching them around wouldn't really work. Thus, if more and more of these resources with minimal use are put toward a different product, they'll become less productive and thus incur a higher [[Opportunity Cost]].
